# CloseBot Business Pricing: Business vs Agency Plans, Message Costs, and the CRM Bill Behind Both

If you typed "CloseBot business pricing" into a search bar, the question behind the keyword is probably not "what is CloseBot" — it's "what will this actually cost me per month, and which of these plans am I supposed to pick?"

The honest answer takes about four minutes, because CloseBot's pricing page is structurally simple and financially less so. There's a free tier, a plan called Core that splits into two different products depending on whether you're a business or an agency, and a custom-quoted Growth tier. Underneath the subscription sits a per-message cost that behaves differently on each track, plus the CRM you're probably already paying for.

## The part the headline price hides: Business pricing scales with message volume

The $64 entry price isn't a fixed subscription so much as a floor. On the Business track, CloseBot's plans page runs a message-volume slider from 100 replies a month up to 100K+, and the price moves with it. Message costs are included in the base price rather than metered on top — which is genuinely unusual for this category — but you pay for the ceiling you choose.

A 2026 review of CloseBot by SetSmart recorded the slider tiers as follows (these come from that review's reading of the plans page, not from CloseBot's help docs, so treat them as a snapshot rather than a contract):

| Messages included per month | Business price (billed monthly) |
| --- | --- |
| 100–500 | $64 |
| 1,000 | $84 |
| 2,000 | $109 |
| 5,000 | $176 |
| 20,000 | $454 |
| 50,000 | $806 |
| 100,000 | roughly $1,059 |

One caution before you budget from that table: CloseBot's own help center still lists the Business track in job-flow terms — $64/mo for 1 job flow, $197/mo for 3, $297/mo for 10, $397/mo for unlimited. A June 2025 agency guide describes the same four tiers as agent counts. The live plans page now leads with the message-volume slider. Two official sources describing the same product differently is a good reason to confirm your exact tier inside the account before your card is charged.

What's consistent across all of it: a paid Business plan comes with a 500-message monthly ceiling included in the price, and you can raise that ceiling (and get better bulk rates) by paying more per month. Blow past the ceiling and overage is billed per message at roughly 2x the normal rate, drawn from a wallet.

## Everything that sits on top of the Business price

The subscription is not the whole bill. Here's what else CloseBot charges, per its own plans page and help documentation:

- **Extra user seats.** One is included. Additional seats are **$5 each**. On the Agency track you can mark that up when you bill clients.
- **Extra storage.** Business plans include 1 MB of knowledge-library storage. Additional storage runs **$0.10 to $3.00 per MB per month**, cheaper the more you buy. For scale, CloseBot describes 1 MB of text as roughly 1,000 pages.
- **Additional agents.** One agent can serve unlimited accounts inside a single niche. More agents for more industries cost extra.
- **Knowledge uploads are billed on text size.** If you upload a video, CloseBot bills the transcript — not the file.
- **A "message" is a segment.** The plans page states 1 message = 1 segment, unless you switch on the Agent Node's unlimited potential (many tools, oversized instructions). Then billing moves to token costs and a single message can burn several segments. Budget loosely if you plan to run heavy agents.

Two more items that show up on the invoice side rather than the pricing page: there are **no refunds**, and the money you spend on the AI model itself depends on how your account is configured. Which brings up an inconsistency worth flagging.

CloseBot's plans page FAQ says the company does not allow "bring your own key" because of security concerns — implying model costs are baked in. The help center article about V2 says the opposite: that V2 requires you to use your own API keys and that CloseBot does not cover provider costs (Anthropic, OpenAI, and so on). Older CloseBot pricing posts work through token costs of roughly $0.0025 to $0.01 per message depending on the provider. If your business runs meaningful volume, that difference is not rounding error. Ask about it on the demo call before you commit to a tier.

## The Agency plan: $397 a month and a different economic model

The Agency track exists for one reason: so you can sell AI appointment setting to clients and keep a margin. The headline number is $397/month, and the mechanism is re-billing.

On the current plans page, CloseBot states agencies are billed a flat **$0.012 per message** that they can re-bill to clients. The help center describes it as **$0.006 per message** with markup of your choosing, plus **$0.006 per MB per day** for storage — and a 2025 CloseBot blog post works through case studies at that $0.006 rate. Same company, three different cost bases. The safest reading: the rate has moved over time and the plans page is the most current statement, but you should confirm the live number in your account because it determines your entire margin.

What you get for the $397 regardless of rate:

- Unlimited agents across unlimited client sources, all from one dashboard
- White-label client portal, so clients see your brand
- Client wallets that top up through your Stripe account — client wallet payments route to you, your wallet payments route to CloseBot
- Re-billing on messages, storage and seats, each with your own markup
- One included seat, $5 per additional seat

SetSmart's review frames the Agency plan as best in class for reselling, and notes the same structural trade-off CloseBot's own materials are upfront about: CloseBot does not connect to Instagram, WhatsApp or Messenger directly. It connects to your CRM, and answers the text channels wired into that CRM. If your client conversations live in a CRM inbox, that's fine. If your leads only ever message you on Instagram and you don't run a CRM, you're buying a CRM to run the AI.

For agencies, though, the math is the pitch. CloseBot cites polled agencies billing an average of $500 per client per month against a $64-plus-usage cost base on the Business side, and the plans page includes a profitability calculator built around that spread.

## Free plan and trial: how to test this without paying

There are two no-cost paths, and they stack:

1. **The free plan** — $0 forever, capped at 100 messages a month, no credit card. Unlimited account connections, 1 agent, 1 seat, 1 MB storage. Over that 100-message cap you can pay as you go at $0.08 per message, but if you're hitting that regularly, a paid tier is cheaper.
2. **A 7-day trial on any paid plan** — including the Agency plan, so you can test white-labeling and re-billing before being billed. Plans are month to month with no contract, and you can upgrade, downgrade or cancel whenever.

Because refunds aren't offered, that 7-day window is your only exit door. Use it on real leads, not a demo bot.

There's also an official discount. CloseBot's own blog lists one code it maintains: **`CLOSEBOT100OFF`**, which takes $100 off your first payment and works on Business and Agency plans. CloseBot explicitly warns that third-party "coupon" pages often carry expired or scraped codes. Separately, annual billing effectively gives you two months free, which is where that $53/mo business entry price and the ~$331/mo agency equivalent come from.

## The cost everyone forgets: your CRM

CloseBot is an add-on, not a standalone product. It integrates natively with HubSpot and HighLevel, plus LeadConnector and custom CRMs, and it automates the conversations already flowing through those systems.

So the real monthly figure looks like CloseBot plus your CRM. SetSmart's August 2026 review gives a concrete example: a solo business wanting around 1,000 AI messages a month ends up near **$181/month** — $84 for CloseBot plus a $97 HighLevel Starter plan — before any per-message channel fees. That's not an argument against CloseBot's pricing; the add-on itself is competitively cheap. It's an argument for reading the total line before you compare it against a tool that connects straight to your channels.

## Business or Agency? A quick way to decide

Two questions settle it.

**Are you billing clients for the AI?** If yes, the Agency plan's white-label portal, client wallets and re-billing turn the subscription into a cost of goods rather than an expense. At $397/month plus $0.012 per message, you need a re-billing model that clears that — which the average $500-per-client billing figure suggests is normal, not aspirational.

**What volume do you actually run?** Under 100 messages a month, stay free. Around 500 to a few thousand, the Business track's included message costs are hard to beat: at $64 to $176 a month you're paying for capacity, not for each reply. That predictability matters more than the headline rate once your lead flow gets lumpy.

What you don't need is Growth. HIPAA compliance, quarterly audits, 99.99% priority uptime and SLAs are for regulated or high-volume operations. If you're not sure whether you need it, you don't.

A note on quality signals, since price questions usually hide a value question: G2's CloseBot listing shows **4.8 out of 5 stars** across 124 reviews, with reviewers consistently pointing to fast setup and conversation automation. CloseBot's own numbers — 1M+ booked appointments, 150k+ daily messages, 1,000+ agencies — are vendor figures, not audited ones. The pattern is consistent with a mature product rather than a launch-week pitch.

## Questions people ask before paying

**Is CloseBot's free plan actually free forever?**
Yes, as long as you stay at or under 100 messages a month. No credit card, no trial clock.

**Can I switch from Business to Agency later?**
Yes. CloseBot notes that upgrading is straightforward, and that even the 7-day Agency trial gives you visibility into re-billing and white-labeling, which the Business plan does not.

**Does the coupon work on the Agency plan?**
CloseBot states `CLOSEBOT100OFF` applies to both Business and Agency plans.

**Do I need a contract?**
No. Outside the Growth tier, plans run month to month.

**What happens if I go over my message ceiling?**
Business plans bill per message at a 2x overage rate from a wallet. Agencies can re-bill usage to clients with markup.

**Is the Agency plan only worth it at high volume?**
Not necessarily. An agency with four sub-accounts and low message volume can still come out ahead on re-billing structure alone — one CloseBot case study puts a beginner-stage account at roughly $403/month all-in, with the base plan making up almost all of it. At that scale, you're paying for the re-billing and white-label machinery, not the messages.
